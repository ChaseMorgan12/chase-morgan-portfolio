Sprint one has come and gone and with it comes an excellent frame to hold our game within. This sprint turned out very well for us and if we go at the pace we are going at currently, we will definitely finish all of our cards with time for stretch goals. As the lead programmer, the job that was assigned to me was developing the main portal mechanic of the game.

This sprint turned out great for me as I was able to get all cards assigned to me at kick off done plus more that I asked for after the kickoff. Most cards were related to the main portal mechanic as it was deemed an epic which was the right move as it was split into around ten cards with around two to three hours of work for each one. I wanted to make this mechanic as close as possible to Valve's Portal portals while still staying in performance ballpark of mobile devices. I had to think about the most performant, visually attracting, and fun way to implement the portals.

Around 75 percent of this sprint was taken up by portals with the other 25 percent being level loading and obstacles. The biggest problems that I faced when making the portals was the perspective as this game supports an isometric view which can be a problem when placing portals at weird angles. I didn't really have an answer to this and am going to be experimenting with outside playtesting hopefully during this in sprint two or three. Otherwise, the portal mechanic was very straight forward and easy to make with the most time spent linking portals and making sure they can be seen through.
At first, I started with the placement of objects in an isometric view which proved more difficult than I thought it would be as I had to account for touchscreen input and mouse input for quick testing in the editor on PC and android. Once I had that done I moved onto the plane projection needed for the portals to be placed in scene which took me a while only because I rewrote the system numerous times before finishing as there seemed to be always a glaring issue with almost every attempt. Next, I linked the portals and made it so you can look through the portals which the only hard part was working with render textures which was confusing at first. Then was teleportation which was done in a four-step process where at first the crate becomes a trigger, then once it exits the first portal it will teleport to the next one, then it will receive normal collisions, and finally the cube is done so clean up and make sure all values are reset back to normal. The last big thing that the portals needed was to save the momentum of the crate which all it does is find out what wall the crate is going through and apply that momentum to whatever new vector it needs. It also makes sure the crate has enough momentum to get through the portals before teleporting preventing the crate from getting stuck.

Level loading was another big thing that was assigned to me over this sprint as it will allow us to quickly get into building numerous levels and see how each one flows into one another. The system that I implemented allows quick loading through prefab instantiation and has an animation of the level building itself to make it look a bit more polished. This was done with interpolation and just goes down the list of objects in the level prefab skipping over any that are marked to not animate in on level load. This also came with a level reset which stores all states of the level when it was first made and then returns it to that state if the player dies.

The last thing assigned to me in this sprint were obstacles that will kill the player upon death. This required the level system having the ability to restart which was not done at the time I got this card assigned so I had to ask for a card to do that before moving onto obstacles. After that was finished, I had an easy time making it so that if the player collides with the obstacle, the obstacle will invoke a method that will kill the player. I programmed it like that as the player object does not need to know about the obstacles existence which helps keep the code decoupled.

Overall, I believe that this sprint went swimmingly with everyone doing an appropriate amount of work. I am very excited to begin my work on sprint two and cannot wait to see how the game turns out!
